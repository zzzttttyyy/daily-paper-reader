# Score Mismatch and Set-Constrained Sampling in Zero-Shot Conditional Discrete Diffusion 

Renxiang Huang †, Yuchen Liang †, Yingbin Liang, Ness Shroff, and Lifeng Lai 

Abstract 

Discrete diffusion models have become a powerful framework for modeling complex discrete distri-butions across a range of applications. While theoretical convergence guarantees depend on consistency between the target and training distributions, such a case rarely holds in practice, making score mismatch a central challenge in discrete diffusion sampling. This is particularly relevant to zero-shot conditional generation, where a model trained on the unconditional distribution is directly reused at inference time to sample from a conditional target distribution. In this paper, we provide the first theoretical analysis for score-mismatched discrete diffusion models and derive a general KL divergence upper bound that clearly identifies the additional bias caused by such a mismatch. Our analysis covers partial-observation constraints, including text infilling, sentence completion, and more general coordinatewise set constraints. Specifically, we analyze the classical Conditional SEDD sampler and show that it can incur a bias term that scales with the dimension, vocabulary size, and compatibility. Based on this analysis, we propose a Randomized Hard Clamping (RHC) sampler for coordinatewise set constraints. We show that the assignment probabilities can be expressed exactly by the unconditional scores with absorbing rate. Finally, numerical and real-data experiments demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed RHC sampler. 

Index Terms 

Convergence analysis, discrete diffusion, score mismatch, set constraints, zero-shot conditional generation. 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

In recent years, diffusion model has become one of the core paradigms in generative modeling and has achieved impressive success in image, audio, and text generation. Compared with the classical autoregressive (AR) models, diffusion models model the complex data distribution through gradual denoising, demonstrating the notable advantages in generation quality, stability, and flexibility [1], [2]. For discrete data such as text sequences, discrete diffusion models extend this denoising principle to the discrete case by defining the noising and denoising processes directly on the discrete state space [3], [4]. In an idealized setting, diffusion models are trained and evaluated on the same underlying distribution. However, in practice, this assumption often fails, as discrepancies between the training and target distributions can arise due to factors such as privacy constraints, computational limitations, and mismatches between the training setup and the intended sampling task [5]–[7]. A representative example is conditional generation, where part of the data is observed and the

goal is to infer the unobserved part. Many tasks involving discrete data fall into this category, including sequence completion and text infilling [8]–[11]. Such tasks include generating the subsequent content based on the given prefix and filling the missing content while preserving the context. We refer to conditioning on fixed values as sampling “under fixed observations.” More generally, each constrained coordinate may admit several candidate values, which leads to sampling under the coordinate wise set constraints. We call a feasible combination of values an “assignment”. For example, in text rewriting and lexical simplification, each position of the word can be associated with a set of candidate replacements [12], [13]. Fixed observations are recovered when each set contains a single value. Compared to AR models, diffusion models are more naturally suited to these tasks, as they do not rely on a fixed generation order and can flexibly handle partially observed inputs. Within conditional sampling, one solution is to train a dedicated conditional diffusion model or adapt a pretrained model for each task, such as predictor-free guidance [14] and inference-time alignment methods [15]. However, such approaches may be costly because they require additional training of a new conditional model beyond unconditional pretraining. In comparison, zero-shot conditional generation does not require such an additional conditional model, since it directly uses the given condition at inference time to build a conditional sampler [9], [10]. The core challenge for zero-shot conditional samplers is the inherent mismatch between the pretrained unconditional model and the conditional model needed in the inference process. In diffusion sampling, this discrepancy manifests in the score function, a phenomenon known as 

score mismatch [7]. Among all zero-shot conditional samplers, the Conditional SEDD method [9] is particularly relevant to this paper. At each diffusion sampling step, Conditional SEDD enforces the fixed observations by setting the corresponding components of the current sample to their observed values, an operation we refer to as “hard clamping.” Although this method is straightforward to implement in practice, we will show that it is not generally equivalent to the true conditional reverse process, thereby introducing bias. Moreover, this approach still lacks any theoretical justifications. This naturally raises the first question studied in this paper: 

Question 1: Can we characterize the bias induced by score-mismatched discrete diffusion models? 

Moving from fixed observations to set constraints introduces another challenge, that is, how to allocate sampling probabilities to each feasible assignment. Once an assignment is selected, the fixed-observation sampler can be reused to generate the remaining contents. After under-standing the construction of the assignment distribution, in order to make the fixed-observation sampler better aligned with the target distribution given set constraints while preserving the main advantage of the zero-shot approach, the second question arises: 

Question 2: Can we design a novel method that extends the fixed-observation sampler to the set constraints setting, while explicitly controlling the additional approximation error? 

In this paper, we answer both questions affirmatively. 

A. Our Contributions 

Overall, our analysis focuses on how mismatched scores interfere with the reverse rate matrix, thereby introducing bias into the sampling process for both general and zero-shot conditional samplers. This analysis provides the convergence guarantee for each assignment in set constraints. Based on the mixture representation of the assignments, we further extend the analysis from fixed observations to general set constraints. The detailed contributions of this paper are: 3

Theoretical Guarantees on General Score-Mismatched Discrete Diffusion Models : Upon characterizing the score mismatch for discrete diffusion models, we provide the first non-asymptotic convergence upper bound in KL-divergence between the target (mismatched) and the sampling distributions. Beyond the intrinsic sampling error when there is no mismatch, our theory characterizes a bias term induced by the score mismatch that arises from using the score of the training distribution to sample from a target (mismatched) distribution. Unlike the analysis in the continuous diffusion setting where the mismatch is expressed through the perturbation in the reverse Gaussian mean [7], the mismatch for our setting perturbs the reverse rate matrix of the continuous time Markov chain (CTMC). Moreover, the Bregman divergence term associated with score entropy differs from the usual L2 error for the continuous setting, introducing additional complications in the analysis. 

Bias Characterization of Conditional SEDD: Based on the general score mismatch analysis, we further provide the first theoretical guarantees for the classical Conditional SEDD algorithm [9] under a fixed observation. By comparing the hard clamp score with the true conditional score, we reveal that the effect of hard clamping depends on the diffusion process. Under the absorbing rate, these two scores are identical on unobserved coordinates given the fixed non-MASK observation, and thus the convergence bound does not contain an additional score-mismatch bias term. Under the uniform rate, the hard clamping can introduce score mismatch over unobserved coordinates. With the assumption of condition compatibility, we bound this bias and characterize the dependence on the unobserved coordinates, vocabulary size, and compatibility parameter. 

Randomized Hard Clamping (RHC) for Set Constraints: We propose a Randomized Hard Clamping (RHC) sampler for the set-constraint setting. In particular, we consider the setting where each constrained index in ¯Ω admits several candidate values from the admissible sets rather than a single fixed value. As a result, the target conditional distribution admits an exact mixture representation according to the assignments. We further show that the exact assignment distribution can be reconstructed based on the unconditional score for absorbing-rate CTMCs. As a result, the proposed RHC sampler achieves zero-shot generation with a pretrained unconditional discrete diffusion model. The numerical simulations and real-data experiments further support our theoretical results and validate the practical effectiveness of the proposed RHC sampler. 

B. Related Works 

In this section, we focus on related works for convergence analyses and conditional genera-tion for discrete diffusion models. Other related works on conditional continuous diffusion are provided in Appendix I. 

Score-matched Discrete Diffusion Model: The existing discrete diffusion analysis focuses on the score-matched setting, that is, the target distribution is consistent with the training distribution. In particular, several recent works have established non-asymptotic convergence guarantees under different noise and sampling schemes [16]–[20]. However, these results cannot be directly applied when the target distribution differs from the training distribution, as in the zero-shot conditional generation. 

Guided Discrete Diffusion Model: Recent works have studied guidance mechanisms in discrete diffusion. [14] proposed predictor guidance and predictor-free guidance applicable to CTMC-based discrete diffusion. Their method realized the guidance through scaling the rate matrix. [21] directly derived the classifier-based and classifier-free guidance rules by reweighting the transition kernel in discrete diffusion. 4

For conditional sampling at large, there are several strands of work. [22] proposed a discrete training-free guidance framework. [23] extended the training-free guidance to multimodal data. [24] pointed out that the bias exists in common discrete guidance methods and adopted Sequential Monte Carlo for debiasing. In addition, [25] studied reward optimization in the discrete diffusion. Similarly, [26] proposed soft value-based decoding, which introduced the soft value function to guide the model to approximate the optimal policy to implement conditional generation. These studies focus on how to incorporate conditional information into the sampling pro-cess. Our work provides a general convergence framework to explicitly quantify the additional sampling error introduced by the mismatch. 

Zero-Shot Discrete Diffusion Model: Zero-shot sampling has recently been explored in the discrete diffusion model. SEDD and RADD support zero-shot text infilling by fixing the prompt at each sampling step instead of retraining for the condition [9], [27]. A discrete diffusion model based on heat bath dynamics, developed by [10], reported good performance in zero-shot tasks, such as text and image infilling. [28] proposed a training-free method to calibrate the confidence of the sequence length to allow the adaptive length of the generated sequence. [29] proposed a plug-and-play posterior sampling method based on split Gibbs sampling. Their method introduces external conditions through the likelihood or reward guidance. Finally, note that all the works mentioned above focused mainly on algorithmic design and empirical performance, but did not characterize the theoretical performance introduced by modifications to the sampling process. Our work establishes non-asymptotic convergence guarantees to quantify how the score mis-match affects the convergence of zero-shot conditional sampling with absorbing and uniform rates. This framework further supports the analysis for set constraints. 

Constrained Diffusion Models: There are several studies regarding the explicit constraint for diffusion models. In continuous diffusion, [30] formulate the sampling process as an optimization problem to guide the generation result to satisfy the constraints. Using a similar idea, [31] introduced Constrained Discrete Diffusion. They incorporated differentiable projection and the augmented Lagrangian method into the sampling process so that the generated samples satisfy the conditional constraints. On the application side, [32] proposed a constrained decoding method applicable to the diffusion language model, which aims to make the generation result conform to the context-free grammar. For set constraints, our RHC uses the unconditional score to assign probabilities to feasible values. The convergence guarantee separates the assignment distribution error and the sampling error. II. The score mismatch is naturally a distortion of the original reverse jump rate. This is an important difference from the additive score mismatch, which is typical for continuous diffusion models [7]. For the continuous models, the score mismatch can be expressed as an additive perturbation in the Gaussian reverse mean, that is, the difference between the posterior means. In contrast, for discrete diffusion models, the reverse process is driven by the rate matrix, where the change of the target distribution will result in a modification of these ratios rather than an additive perturbation. This motivates defining score mismatch also in terms of a ratio. 

C. Zero-Shot Conditional Sampling under Fixed Observations 

In the above general score mismatch setting, the target distribution ˜q0 differs from the training distribution q0. A crucial scenario of this setting is conditional generation, such as text infilling [9], [27]. For this scenario, we do not aim to sample from the unconditional distribution q0;instead, our goal is to complete the unobserved parts of a sequence given the condition of observing the partial sequence. More specifically, let x0 ∈ [S]d be divided into two parts: x0 =[xΩ0 , x ¯Ω0 ], where Ω refers to the set of unobserved indices that need to be filled and ¯Ω refers to the observed indices. Given a non-MASK observation y ∈ [S]| ¯Ω|, the target distribution is the conditional distribution of the unobserved coordinates given the observation y:

˜q0 = qΩ0|y = q0(xΩ0 |x¯Ω0 = y).

In the zero-shot setting, our goal is to sample from the conditional target using a pretrained unconditional discrete diffusion without any additional training for the given observations.

In this paper, we have provided the convergence guarantees for the general score-mismatched discrete diffusion model. Based on the general results, we have further studied the zero-shot conditional generation. Our analysis characterizes the bias of Conditional SEDD under the fixed observations. We have shown that the hard clamp score coincides with the true conditional score under the absorbing rate case but not under the uniform rate case. We have derived an additional score-mismatched bias term in the KL upper bound under the uniform rate. To further extend conditional sampling to coordinate-wise set constraints, we have designed a novel Randomized Hard Clamping sampler with an absorbing rate. A natural direction for future work is to extend the current analysis and samplers to more general multimodal data and more complex constraint information. REFERENCES

Conditional Continuous Diffusion Model 

There exists sufficient research that focuses on the conditional generation related to the continuous diffusion model. These studies are primarily divided into two fields. One field focuses mainly on the guidance mechanism. For example, [35] proposed classifier guidance, which utilized an external classifier to modify the score function to inject conditional information into the diffusion model. [36] introduced classifier-free guidance, which combined an unconditional diffusion and a conditional diffusion to achieve conditional generation and eliminate the classifier. [37] introduced the CLIP model to measure the similarity between images and text, which can therefore be used as external guidance in diffusion models. [38] further studied the guidance mechanism in text-to-image generation and compared CLIP guidance and classifier-free guidance. [39] modified the score function for the image editing area through the CLIP guidance and used the unconditional diffusion for the non-editing area. 23 

Another field is based on the unconditional diffusion without extra training. SDEdit [40] realized the image synthesis and editing by noising and denoising the user input. This method was trained on unlabeled data without retraining on the new input or tasks. RePaint [41] fixed the noised pixels to constrain the sampling process and achieve conditional generation. [42] theoretically analyzed the RePaint [41] and proposed RePaint+ to correct the bias induced by RePaint [41]. [43] proposed the ILVR method to implement image generation given reference images. This method refined the latent variables in the unconditional process to approximate the conditional process. DDRM in [44] solved the linear inverse problem by the posterior sampling based on a pretrained diffusion model, and further DPS in [45] extended the posterior sampling method to the general inverse problem. DDNM and DDNM+ [46] are also well-known zero-shot frameworks that only rely on the pretrained diffusion model. For the theoretical works, [7] provided the theoretical guarantee of the zero-shot conditional diffusion model by introducing the score mismatch concept under the continuous state cases. In addition, [47]–[49] designed the specialized conditional frameworks in the diffusion model to implement the conditional generation rather than to solely control the inference process.
