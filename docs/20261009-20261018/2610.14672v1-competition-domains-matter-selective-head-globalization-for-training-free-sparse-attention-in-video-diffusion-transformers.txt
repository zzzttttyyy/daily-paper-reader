# Competition Domains Matter: Selective Head Globalization for Training-Free Sparse Attention in Video Diffusion Transformers 

## Tiantian Liu, Meng Wan, Zhiqiang Liang, Jue Wang ∗, Ningming Nie, Yangang Wang ∗

Abstract 

Training-free sparse attention is a practical route to efficient video diffusion transformer inference, yet existing methods typically fix the competition domain at each query row for every attention head. Prior work primarily addresses how to estimate token-level importance within this fixed domain; whether the domain itself should adapt to head heterogeneity remains under-specified. We introduce EG-Sparse , a training-free framework that routes low-entropy heads to a grouped cross-head pool while retaining per-row local selection for high-entropy heads, using only scores already materialized by the sparse pipeline. A controlled diagnostic fixes the ranking score and varies only the competition rule: grouped cross-head selection reduces low-entropy-head reconstruction er-ror by 9.8% , whereas forcing high-entropy heads into an all-head global pool increases it by 7.9% . Across four configura-tions, EG-Sparse achieves higher video quality while reduc-ing attention density and GPU denoising latency compared with strong training-free sparse-attention baselines. Mecha-nism ablations further show that selective domain adaptation outperforms uniform, random, and fixed domain-assignment policies. These results support competition-domain adapta-tion as a distinct design axis within centroid-guided training-free sparse attention. 

## 1 Introduction 

Video diffusion transformers (DiTs) (Peebles and Xie 2023) have become a central backbone of modern video generation, powering state-of-the-art models such as CogVideoX (Yang et al. 2025b), HunyuanVideo (Kong et al. 2025), and Wan (Team Wan 2025). Recent systems further extend this paradigm toward scalable and long-form video synthe-sis (Gupta et al. 2024; Jin et al. 2025; Lin et al. 2025b; Henschel et al. 2025). As these systems scale toward longer videos and higher resolutions, the quadratic complexity of full 3D attention becomes the dominant inference bottleneck. Efficiency research around diffusion transformers spans architecture/token-level compression (Sun et al. 2025; Tian et al. 2024; Wang et al. 2025b; Chang et al. 2025), inter-step reuse (Ma et al. 2024; Zhao et al. 2025b; Liu et al. 2025; Lv et al. 2025), quantization and distributed execution (Li et al. 2025a; 2024), attention-kernel optimization (Shah et al. 2024; Zhang et al. 2025c; b; Wang et al. 2025a), and head compression (Zhang et al. 2025a; e). Our focus is distinct: the competition domain used to select interactions within a centroid-guided sparse-attention pipeline. Training-free sparse attention has emerged as a practical remedy: it selectively computes only a subset of query-key interactions at inference time without modifying pretrained weights (Xi et al. 2025; Yang et al. 2025a; Zhang et al. 2025d; Xu et al. 2025). Within training-free sparse attention, top-p block se-lection has become the dominant paradigm, adopted by SVG2 (Yang et al. 2025a), XAttention (Xu et al. 2025), SpargeAttn (Zhang et al. 2025d), and others. The common workflow first partitions tokens into clusters, then ranks can-didate (query-cluster, key-cluster) pairs by some importance score, and retains pairs until their cumulative importance reaches a threshold p. Despite extensive work on improving the ranking (how to estimate block importance more accu-rately), the partition and retention steps share an unexamined assumption: the competition domain, i.e., the set of candi-date pairs that compete for the same budget, is always each head independently and within each head, each query row independently. This uniform policy ignores substantial head heterogene-ity in video DiTs. Some heads concentrate most probabil-ity mass on a few key clusters, whereas others distribute it broadly. Figure 1 shows this variation across layers and heads and contrasts two dense attention structures from the same layer. Normalized entropy varies substantially at both scales: one representative head concentrates on sparse cluster pairs, whereas the other spreads mass broadly across keys. The value of enlarging the competition domain should therefore depend on head structure. A controlled diagnostic (Section 3.3) fixes the ranking score and varies only the domain. Low-entropy heads benefit from larger pools, whereas high-entropy heads are penalized in an all-head global pool where concentrated heads cap-ture disproportionate budget. Fixed-domain policies there-fore cannot satisfy both groups simultaneously. To turn this analysis into online competition-domain adap-tation, we propose EG-Sparse (Entropy-Guided Sparse At-tention). As shown in figure 2, at each denoising step, it computes per-head entropy from centroid attention already materialized by the base pipeline. Low-entropy heads enter a grouped cross-head pool while the remainder retain per-row local selection. Our contributions are as follows: 1. We identify the competition-domain mismatch : a con-trolled diagnostic at fixed ranking score reveals that head heterogeneity determines opposing domain needs, where low-entropy heads benefit from cross-head budget shar-ing while high-entropy heads suffer interference when competing in the same pool. 2. We propose EG-Sparse , a training-free online frame-work that selectively expands the competition domain to cross-head global selection for low-entropy heads while retaining per-row local selection for high-entropy heads. 3. We demonstrate consistent quality–efficiency improve-ments over strong baselines across four model configura-tions. Mechanism ablations validate that entropy-guided selective domain adaptation outperforms uniform, ran-dom and fixed domain-assignment policies. 

## 2 Related Work 

Video-DiT sparse attention. Sparse VideoGen (SVG) profiles spatio-temporal head patterns (Xi et al. 2025); SVG2 adds semantic token clustering and centroid Top-

p selection (Yang et al. 2025a); XAttention uses anti-diagonal block scores (Xu et al. 2025); and SpargeAttn applies selective token compression (Zhang et al. 2025d). Sparse-vDiT and Compact Attention search offline for struc-tured, model-specific sparse patterns (Chen et al. 2026; Li et al. 2025b), whereas EG-Sparse routes heads online from current-layer entropy without offline profiling. Other video methods change sliding-tile locality or cached search (Zhang et al. 2025f; Xia et al. 2025), structured topology or to-ken order (Li et al. 2025c; Shmilovich et al. 2025; Zhao et al. 2025a), and low-resolution guidance or progressive resolution (Shen et al. 2025; Zhang et al. 2025g). These approaches primarily change how candidates are found or executed; EG-Sparse changes which candidates share a Top-

p budget. 

Head adaptation and cross-domain context. Video-DiT methods allocate head-wise computation using training statistics, learned mechanisms, or offline calibration (Wu et al. 2026; Zhang et al. 2025a; Zheng et al. 2026). AdaSpa combines cached search with head-adaptive spar-sity (Xia et al. 2025), whereas HASTE couples temporal reuse with calibrated per-head budgets. EG-Sparse instead uses current-step entropy to decide which heads share a pool, without learned routing or calibration. Entropy has previ-ously been diagnostic (Zhou et al. 2026; Wu et al. 2025); here it predicts domain-dependent efficiency. Head-role and adaptive-selection methods for causal language models (Xiao et al. 2024; Gao et al. 2024; Lin et al. 2025a; Zhu et al. 2025) assume different masks and inference dynamics, so they provide conceptual context rather than direct video-DiT baselines.

Why entropy is useful for domain assignment. Entropy describes the shape of the score landscape before sparse se-lection. As we demonstrate in the con-trolled diagnostic below, this difference determines whether a head benefits from or is harmed by a larger competition domain. Entropy is therefore used not as a semantic head label, but as an online routing signal for domain assignment.

The competition-domain diagnostic in Section 3 uses atten-tion mass P ′ to isolate the domain effect. In the deployed method, the cross-head pool replaces P ′ with a value-aware importance score:

## 4 Experiments 

Our evaluation is organized around three questions: (i) Does EG-Sparse improve quality–efficiency across the evaluated video-DiT configurations (§4)? (ii) Does the competition-domain mismatch diagnosed in Section 3 translate to practi-cal gains, and is selective domain expansion necessary (§4)? (iii) What overhead does online domain adaptation introduce (§4)? 

Experimental Setup 

Models and data. Main Results: Fidelity and Efficiency 

Table 1 shows EG-Sparse improves fidelity and efficiency across model families and conditioning modes. Across all four configurations, EG-Sparse achieves the best PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, and VBench among the compared sparse methods while also attaining the lowest attention density and GPU denoising latency, with 2.03 ×–2.69 × speedup over Dense. This lower-density, higher-quality result shows that the gain does not come from retaining more interactions; in-stead, domain adaptation allocates the sparse-attention bud-get more effectively across rows and heads according to their structural needs. On HunyuanVideo, the PSNR and VBench margins over the strongest competing sparse baseline are larger at 720P than at 480P, suggesting that the benefit may become more pronounced as spatial resolution and attention demands increase. The following mechanism analysis fur-ther isolates the contributions of selective domain routing and value-aware ranking.

## 5 Conclusion 

We revisited a design choice shared by training-free sparse attention methods for video diffusion transformers: fixing sparse competition independently within every query row. A controlled diagnostic at fixed ranking score shows that the cost of this uniform boundary is head-dependent and asymmetric: low-entropy heads benefit monotonically from larger competition domains, while high-entropy heads are penalized when forced into the same pool. EG-Sparse adapts domains online, using current head en-tropy at every layer and denoising step to route low-entropy heads to a grouped cross-head pool while retaining per-row local selection for the rest. The median rule requires neither retraining, offline calibration, nor manual threshold selection. Across the evaluated bidirectional video DiTs, this selective expansion reduces attention density and GPU denoising la-tency while improving overall dense-reference fidelity and generation quality; its behavior in causal or non-diffusion Transformers is untested. Ablations show that the gain is not explained by uniform expansion or an arbitrary split: it depends on directing expansion toward heads predicted to benefit. The broader implication is that video-DiT sparse atten-tion should optimize not only the score and budget, but also the domain over which candidates compete. Together, these findings support competition-domain adaptation as a useful design principle for training-free sparse attention in video DiTs.
