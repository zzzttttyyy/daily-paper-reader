## ABSTRACT 

Reusing Transformer blocks offers a way to increase diffusion-model computation at test time, but naive recurrence can degrade generation beyond its training depth. We introduce LoopREG, a representation-supervised recurrent diffusion Trans-former that repeatedly applies a shared block at the state supervised by a pretrained visual encoder. Alignment is applied after the final recurrent visit during training; inference extends the same learned operator to additional visits. On ImageNet 

256 × 256 , LoopREG reduces SiT-B/2 FID from the local REG baseline’s 15.08 to 4.91 at 400K training steps, a 67.41% reduction. Within a checkpoint trained with two visits, one extra inference visit improves FID from 14.71 to 5.34 with unchanged weights. The benefit persists on SiT-L/2 and SiT-XL/2. Controlled interventions show that useful recurrence follows the location of representation supervision, while REPA alignment produces a smaller extrapolation benefit. ## 1 INTRODUCTION 

Diffusion Transformers such as DiT and SiT devote a fixed stack of blocks to each denoiser evaluation (Peebles & Xie, 2023; Ma et al., 2024). Increasing the number of sampling steps adds computation across diffusion time. Shared recurrence offers a complementary possibility: spend more computation refining the hidden state within each evaluation, while reusing the same parameters. For this to improve generation, a learned block must remain useful when applied beyond its training horizon. 

What makes recurrent computation reusable beyond its training horizon? Our experiments identify representation supervision as an enabling ingredient. Naively repeating a block in plain SiT yields at most a marginal matched-depth improvement and degrades sharply with an extra inference visit. The same recurrent construction becomes useful under REPA alignment and substantially more effective under REG’s representation entanglement (Yu et al., 2025; Wu et al., 2025). This contrast connects the value of extra computation to how the recurrent state is supervised. We introduce LoopREG , a recurrent diffusion Transformer built on REG. LoopREG reuses one Transformer block at the representation-supervised state, applies alignment after the final training-time visit, and extends that block’s trajectory at inference (Figure 2). It retains REG’s joint image and semantic-token denoising objective. Inference adds visits with unchanged weights and a fixed diffusion sampling schedule. The resulting generation gains are substantial. On ImageNet 256 × 256 at 400K training steps, LoopREG-B/2 reaches FID 4.91, compared with 15.08 for the local REG baseline: a 67.41% reduction (Figure 1). Within a LoopREG checkpoint trained with two visits, a third visit reduces FID from 14.71 to 5.34. Useful extra depth also persists on SiT-L/2 and SiT-XL/2. On XL/2, extra inference depth reaches the matched-depth 400K quality at 220K training steps. The key design choice is where to recur. With supervision fixed at block four, extra recurrence helps at the supervised state and fails at the other tested sites. Moving supervision and recurrence together restores the benefit at other depths. A compact analysis relates this placement to direct supervision of the recurrent endpoint and interprets the finite useful inference horizon. Our main contributions are: 2LoopREG Preprint 

1. LoopREG. We introduce representation-supervised shared recurrence for diffusion Trans-formers, enabling additional inference depth with the same trained parameters. 2. Substantial generation gains. LoopREG lowers SiT-B/2 FID from 15.08 to 4.91, with useful recurrent-depth extrapolation across B/2, L/2, and XL/2. Training-horizon sweeps characterize the resulting quality–depth curves. 3. A supervision-based design principle. Plain SiT/REPA/REG controls and site/depth interventions show that representation supervision and its location determine whether extra recurrent computation is useful in the tested settings. 

## 2 RELATED WORK 

Diffusion and representation supervision. DiT replaces the denoising backbone with a Trans-former, and SiT studies flow and diffusion within a stochastic-interpolant framework (Peebles & Xie, 2023; Ma et al., 2024). REPA aligns projected intermediate states with clean pretrained visual representations (Yu et al., 2025). REG jointly denoises image latents and a semantic token while retaining representation alignment (Wu et al., 2025). SRA instead uses the model’s own EMA representations for alignment (Jiang et al., 2026). We study a further capability of representation supervision: enabling a recurrent operator to improve generation beyond its training horizon. The supervision-site interventions identify where this capability emerges. 

Looped Transformers and latent computation. Universal Transformers combine shared depth with adaptive computation (Dehghani et al., 2019). Theoretical studies show that looped Transformers can implement programs (Giannou et al., 2023), learn iterative data-fitting procedures (Yang et al., 2024), and realize multistep gradient descent in controlled in-context-learning settings (Gatmiry et al., 2024). Recurrent-depth language models explore scaling latent computation beyond a fixed forward pass (Geiping et al., 2025). We investigate when repeated computation improves a diffusion denoiser, using representation supervision as the central intervention. 

Elastic depth and visual generation. LoopFormer conditions recurrent updates on time and step size and trains shortcut-consistent trajectories (Jeddi et al., 2026); PonderNet learns a stopping distribution (Banino et al., 2021). ELT explores looped visual generation with intra-loop self-distillation and reports gains beyond training depth (Goyal et al., 2026). We instead investigate external representation supervision and its location, connecting recurrent-depth extrapolation to supervised-state dynamics. Our RandomLoop control samples terminal horizons from finite uniform distributions; heavy-tailed random horizons are also used in recurrent-depth language-model training (Geiping et al., 2025). 

Dynamics and inference-time reuse. Deep equilibrium models learn implicitly defined fixed points (Bai et al., 2019). Recurrent-dynamics work distinguishes regimes in which extra iterations help, preserve, or degrade predictions (Viakhirev et al., 2026); training-free looping uses damped updates to mitigate failures of naive reapplication (Chen et al., 2026). LoopREG trains the shared operator explicitly and reuses its learned update at inference. Internal Guidance combines intermediate and final denoising predictions to guide sampling (Zhou et al., 2026). LoopREG varies the recurrent depth of a representation-supervised block within each denoiser call. Its generation benefit depends on the supervision location and the inference horizon. 

## 3 LOOP REG 

LoopREG adds shared recurrence to a representation-supervised diffusion Transformer. Its defining choice is to repeat the block whose output receives representation supervision, so that the supervised state is also the state refined by extra inference computation. 3.1 REPRESENTATION -S UPERVISED SHARED RECURRENCE 

Image and representation inputs. Let x0 be the VAE latent of a clean training image, y its class label, and z = ( zcls , z patch ) the frozen DINOv2 features of the same image. Following REG, we 3LoopREG Preprint 

form an image state and a semantic-token state at a common time t ∼ U [0 , 1] :

xt = (1 − t)x0 + tϵ, ct = (1 − t)zcls + tϵ c, (1) where ϵ and ϵc are independent Gaussian noise. The Transformer processes image tokens and the semantic token together, conditioned on t and y, and predicts both velocities. Figure 2 shows how the clean image supplies the latent and the alignment targets. 

Reusing the supervised block. Write the blocks before the recurrent site as H, the shared block as 

Fθ , and the remaining blocks and output projection as Gϕ. At a fixed sampling state ut = ( xt, c t),

h0 = H(ut; t, y ), hk+1 = Fθ (hk; t, y ), (ˆ vxK , ˆvcK ) = Gϕ(hK ; t, y ). (2) The depth K counts all applications of the shared block, including its ordinary first visit. Thus K = 1 

executes it once and K = 3 executes the same module three times. Each visit receives the preceding visit’s state with the same time and class conditioning. LoopREG uses no recurrent-index embedding or per-visit parameters. The supervision depth d counts Transformer blocks in the original backbone. We co-locate the recurrent block at ℓ = d and read the representation after its final recurrent visit. A nonlinear projector P maps that endpoint to the clean teacher features. Alignment is evaluated once per forward pass, so the training objective supervises the endpoint of the learned trajectory. The main B/2 model uses d = ℓ = 4 , and L/2 and XL/2 use d = ℓ = 8 . Extending depth at inference. Generation initializes both x and c from noise. The frozen visual teacher and clean representations are used only during training. Changing Kinfer changes the number of shared-block applications within a call, while preserving all learned parameters and the diffusion time grid. For example, a model trained with Ktrain = 2 can be evaluated with Kinfer = 3 to add one recurrent refinement at every sampling step.

Controlled variants. Plain LoopSiT uses the same recurrence with image-velocity regression alone. LoopREPA adds patch-token alignment with coefficient 0.5 and has no REG semantic-token path or semantic loss. Independent-copy controls replace the shared operator with separately trained blocks. Protocol. We evaluate class-conditional ImageNet at 256 × 256 , using checkpoints trained for 400K optimization steps with global batch 256. B4 improves, while B2, B6, and B8 degrade. Thus matched-depth quality alone does not predict useful extrapolation. We then move supervision with the loop. Representation geometry tracks useful recurrent refinement. We probe the recurrent states of the two LoopREG-B/2 checkpoints on 1,024 forward-noised ImageNet images at five diffusion times. Patch CKA compares mean hidden patch features with mean DINOv2 patch features, using a fixed 256-image subset. Spatial correlation compares pairwise patch-token similarities, using 64 sampled positions across all 1,024 images. These complementary measurements describe semantic organization and spatial structure along the recurrent trajectory. Figure 6 displays every tested depth together with generation FID. For Ktrain = 2 , patch CKA and spatial correlation both peak at Kinfer = 3 , where FID is lowest. Training at Ktrain = 3 moves these geometry peaks to Kinfer = 5 , again matching the best generation depth. The extended trajectory therefore contains states with stronger teacher-referenced geometry than the state at which training ends. This correspondence connects the generation gains to refinement of the representation selected by LoopREG’s supervision. 

The trained projection and extended trajectory play different roles. Native patch-projector cosine reaches its maximum at the trained depth in both checkpoints. The projector thus favors the supervised endpoint, while the best generation states occur farther along the trajectory. This distinction fits the finite-horizon interpretation in Section 3.3: a block can continue to improve task-relevant geometry after reaching the endpoint used by its training objective. Fixed training horizons give LoopREG its strongest peak FID. They also concentrate generation around a narrower depth range: at its best B/2 depth, the Ktrain = 3 model has precision 0.851 and recall 0.452, versus the REG baseline’s 0.649/0.640. RandomLoop broadens the useful range and preserves more coverage. Training with K ∈ { 1, 2, 3} yields a best observed FID of 11.68 with recall 0.640. This offers a choice between peak fidelity and flexibility in the number of inference visits. A timestep-dependent policy adds another operating point. Applying Kinfer = 3 for t ≥ 0.6 and 

Kinfer = 2 for t < 0.6 reaches FID 7.25, between the two fixed-depth results, with recall 0.566. It reduces total block executions by 4.17% relative to fixed Kinfer = 3 . Appendix D reports the complete random-depth curves, compute accounting, and unshared controls. 

## 5 CONCLUSION 

LoopREG makes additional test-time depth useful by repeatedly applying a shared Transformer block at a representation-supervised state. It reduces SiT-B/2 FID from 15.08 to 4.91 at 400K training steps and extends recurrent-depth gains to L/2 and XL/2. The supervision-site interventions identify a transferable design choice within these models: useful recurrence follows the supervised representation. These results establish a practical way to improve diffusion generation by extending learned recurrent computation. 

Scope and future directions. The experiments cover class-conditional ImageNet, SiT backbones, and DINOv2 supervision. Useful depth has a finite optimum, and improved FID often accompanies lower recall. Extending the approach to other generation settings and selecting depths without a generation sweep are natural next steps.

## A EXPERIMENTAL SETUP AND EVIDENCE PROVENANCE 

We study class-conditional ImageNet generation at 256 × 256 . Generation metrics and representation probes are retained in separate tables with their checkpoint, recurrent-depth, and evaluation settings. All numerical tables and inline results are generated from these records. The final 1,024-image DINO probe supplies the representation analysis; output-depth interventions and smaller-sample depth-selection diagnostics use their own evaluation protocols. 

Training. SiT-B/2 has 12 blocks of width 768 with 12 heads; SiT-L/2 has 24 blocks of width 1024 with 16 heads. Image latents have four channels and spatial size 32 × 32 , with latent patch size two. The semantic teacher is DINOv2 ViT-B/14 (Oquab et al., 2023). Training uses global batch 256, AdamW with learning rate 10 −4, betas (0 .9, 0.999) , epsilon 10 −8, no weight decay, gradient clipping at one, class dropout 0.1, FP16 mixed precision, and TF32. The default launcher uses eight GPUs and checkpoints every 10K steps. RandomLoop’s horizon draw is a deterministic function of the experiment seed and optimization step and is synchronized across ranks; equal configured weights implement a uniform distribution over the specified choices. Horizon sampling has no learned halting mechanism. Nominally equal step counts do not match training FLOPs across horizons or supervision methods. 

Sampling. The sampler constructs 250 time points from 1 to 0.04 and appends zero. It applies 249 stochastic Euler–Maruyama updates and a final deterministic drift update, totaling 250 network calls at CFG=1. REG jointly evolves image and semantic-token states. Network inputs/weights are FP32 with TF32 enabled; velocities and state updates are promoted to FP64. The VAE decoder is evaluated separately. Generated images are clamped, multiplied by 255, and converted to uint8 by truncation. The evaluator reads a local ImageNet reference and the local OpenAI TensorFlow Inception graph. FID, sFID, IS, precision, and recall are imported as reported, without recomputing them from unavailable image batches. The configured global seed is zero and the rank seed is seed × world _size + rank . Raw artifacts show different world sizes or per-GPU batches across runs; therefore these are not matched-noise paired comparisons. The source’s resume branch skips the sampler for completed batches and does not advance the random-number stream through their internal SDE noise draws. This prevents a blanket claim of bit-identical resumed and uninterrupted generation. The available artifacts do not establish which final cells, if any, were affected. The paired output-depth experiments below instead use fresh full runs and record their complete random-number traces. 

Late evidence and precision. The final N=1,024 mechanism bundle and unshared-x3 K3/K6 metrics supersede earlier development results or missing-data notes. The L/2 main-result curve is supplied as the experiment owner’s final server-summary Markdown, at four decimal places for FID; it is parsed automatically, but its original metric/configuration JSON is not yet part of this evidence set.
