## Abstract 

Feature caching has become a mainstream training-free strategy for accelerating video diffusion models by reusing intermediate outputs across denoising steps. However, existing adaptive methods rely on heuristic difference measures mis-aligned with actual generation quality, employ a single global threshold that ignores heterogeneous phase dynamics, and never validate their budget estimates against true approximation error, allowing systematic bias to propagate uncorrected. We propose AlignCache , a closed-loop caching framework that directly aligns schedul-ing decisions with their true influence on the final generated video through three tightly coupled components: (1) phase-adaptive budget routing that partitions the denoising trajectory into structurally distinct phases, each governed by an indepen-dent threshold and a staleness-aware guard; (2) closed-loop threshold calibration that exploits counterfactual error measurement at the classifier-free guided output level to adjust decision boundaries online via an EMA feedback loop; and (3) momentum-augmented draft synthesis combining direction-magnitude gated resid-ual extrapolation with anchor-guided unconditional correction. By transforming scheduling from a static risk accumulator into a feedback-driven, self-correcting controller, AlignCache consistently achieves superior acceleration-quality trade-offs across state-of-the-art video generation models. Our code is available at 

https://github.com/jiangjyjy/AlignCache .

## 1 Introduction 

Diffusion models [7, 20, 21] have become the dominant paradigm for high-quality generative mod-eling, with Diffusion Transformers (DiTs) [20] powering state-of-the-art video generation systems such as Open-Sora [34], HunyuanVideo [13], and Wan2.1 [25]. Despite their impressive generation quality, the iterative denoising nature of these models incurs substantial computational costs, dozens of sequential forward passes through billion-parameter networks, making efficient inference a critical bottleneck for deployment and motivating a growing body of training-free acceleration research. Among various training-free strategies, feature caching [14, 18, 33] has emerged as an effective paradigm by exploiting temporal redundancy across denoising steps. Static caching methods [23, 33] adopt fixed intervals but cannot adapt to timestep dynamics, while adaptive approaches [11, 14, 18, 28, 35] use runtime metrics to decide whether to skip or compute. However, existing adaptive methods exhibit three structural limitations: (i) they rely on heuristic difference measures that do not reflect the impact of caching on the generated video; (ii) they employ a single global threshold blind

In this work, we propose AlignCache , a closed-loop caching framework that directly aligns scheduling decisions with their actual influence on the final generated video. Our main contributions are: (1) We identify the misalignment between heuristic caching criteria and generation quality, and propose AlignCache, a closed-loop framework bridging this gap via counterfactual error measurement. (2) We design a phase-adaptive budget routing mechanism with staleness-aware guards and closed-loop threshold calibration, replacing static thresholds with a self-correcting controller. (3) We introduce a momentum-augmented draft synthesis combining residual extrapolation with anchor-guided correction. (4) Experiments on multiple video generation models demonstrate that AlignCache consistently achieves superior acceleration-quality trade-offs. 

## 2 Related Works 

2.1 Video Diffusion Models 

Diffusion models [7, 20, 21] have established themselves as the dominant paradigm for generative modeling. Early video generation efforts built upon U-Net architectures [22] and latent diffusion frameworks [1, 2, 9] to synthesize temporally coherent content. More recently, the community has shifted towards Diffusion Transformers (DiTs) [20] for their superior scalability in modeling complex spatiotemporal dynamics. State-of-the-art DiT-based systems including Sora [4], Open-Sora [34], CogVideoX [30], HunyuanVideo [13], and Wan2.1 [25] have achieved remarkable fidelity and coherence, largely benefiting from Rectified Flow [16] formulations that enable high-quality generation with fewer sampling steps. 2.2 Feature Caching for Diffusion Acceleration 

To accelerate diffusion inference without retraining, various training-free strategies have been ex-plored, including ODE solvers [12, 17, 24], token merging [3, 32, 36], and sparsity-based ap-proaches [26, 29, 31]. Among these, feature caching has emerged as an effective paradigm for video generation by exploiting temporal redundancy across denoising steps. Static caching meth-ods [5, 23, 33] use fixed intervals and cannot adapt to timestep importance, while adaptive caching approaches [6, 11, 14, 18, 28] introduce runtime metrics for dynamic decisions yet rely on heuristic difference measures that do not reflect the impact of caching on generation quality, leading to either conservative caching with limited speedup or aggressive caching with noticeable quality degradation. Recent works [15, 27] address this misalignment through frequency decomposition or higher-order feature forecasting, yet the fundamental gap between caching criteria and perceptual quality remains under-explored. In contrast, AlignCache directly aligns the caching decision criterion with the influence on the generated video via closed-loop counterfactual error measurement, enabling more precise redundancy identification and superior acceleration-quality trade-offs. 

## 3 Preliminaries 

Empirical Analysis of Residual Dynamics. To motivate AlignCache, we analyze how output residuals evolve along the denoising trajectory of Wan2.1-1.3B (Figure 2). Three observations emerge: (i) residual magnitude exhibits markedly heterogeneous dynamics across the early, middle, and late phases, making a single global threshold structurally insufficient; (ii) properly designed correction reduces relative error by 30 -50% over direct reuse, with the largest gap precisely where direct reuse becomes most error-prone; and (iii) direct reuse error varies systematically across conditional, unconditional, and CFG-guided branches, indicating that scheduling decisions made on a single branch cannot reliably predict the distortion injected into the final output. These observations directly motivate our phase-adaptive routing (§4.1), momentum-augmented draft synthesis (§4.3), and closed-loop calibration at the CFG-guided level (§4.2), respectively.

Open-Loop Budget Scheduling and Its Limitations. Existing schedulers [35] frame routing as a cumulative risk test: a scalar budget Bt accumulates a heuristic risk increment per timestep, and a skip is taken whenever Bt < τ for a fixed threshold τ (full update rule in Appendix A.2). This open-loop design has three structural limitations: (i) a global τ cannot accommodate heterogeneous dynamics across early, mid, and late phases; (ii) the absence of a staleness constraint permits unbounded skip runs when inputs change slowly; and (iii) the budget is never validated against approximation error, so systematic bias propagates uncorrected. AlignCache addresses these limitations through phase-adaptive routing, staleness-aware guarding, and closed-loop threshold calibration (§4). 

## 4 Methodology: AlignCache 

We consider CFG sampling over T denoising timesteps. At each timestep t, the model evaluates conditional and unconditional forward passes, with conditional input xt, exact conditional output 

yt, and CFG-guided output gt. AlignCache decides at each t whether to execute a full forward pass (exact ) or reuse cached computations ( skip ). As illustrated in Figure 3, our framework comprises three coupled components: (i) a phase-adaptive budget routing mechanism that assigns phase-specific thresholds and staleness constraints to segments of the denoising trajectory (§4.1); (ii) a closed-loop threshold calibration module that leverages counterfactual observations from exact computations to adjust decision boundaries (§4.2); and (iii) a momentum-augmented draft synthesis strategy that improves skip-mode fidelity, enabling more aggressive caching with quality guarantees (§4.3). Unlike prior open-loop schedulers comparing a cumulative budget against a fixed threshold τ , AlignCache replaces this static decision boundary with a dynamic, phase-aware, and self-correcting threshold

4.2 Closed-Loop Threshold Calibration 

A limitation of open-loop schedulers is that the budget Bt may systematically deviate from the true approximation error, causing excessive skipping or unnecessary exact computations. We close this gap by exploiting each budget-triggered exact computation as a calibration opportunity.

Source of the joint speed-quality improve-ment. A natural concern is whether AlignCache simply tightens the threshold to trade speed for quality. The data argue otherwise: on Wan2.1-1.3B, AlignCache simultaneously reduces la-tency relative to EasyCache ( 57 .5 s vs. 61 .1 s) 

and improves PSNR by over a full dB. Such co-improvement is enabled by two complementary mechanisms acting in concert. The momentum-augmented draft synthesis produces higher-fidelity skip outputs by capturing residual curvature beyond static reuse, directly reducing approximation error along extended skip runs and accounting for a substantial portion of the PSNR gain. The closed-loop calibration further redirects the scheduler toward exact computation when the calibration ratio ¯rp,t exceeds 1, ensuring that residual budget is spent on the most informative timesteps. Inspecting the per-step routing logs, we observe that AlignCache skips aggressively in the middle phase where residuals are smooth and reverts to exact computation in the late phase when feedback signals indicate growing distortion, behavior that an open-loop scheduler with a single global τ structurally cannot reproduce.

## 6 Conclusion and Future Works 

Conclusion. We presented AlignCache , a closed-loop caching framework that addresses three structural limitations of existing adaptive schedulers: heuristic criteria misaligned with generation quality, single global thresholds insensitive to phase dynamics, and uncorrected systematic bias in budget estimation. By coupling phase-adaptive budget routing, counterfactual error measurement at the CFG-guided output level, and momentum-augmented draft synthesis, AlignCache transforms the scheduling paradigm from a static risk accumulator into a feedback-driven, self-correcting controller. Experiments on Wan2.1-1.3B, Open-Sora-1.2, and HunyuanVideo show that AlignCache consistently achieves the best speed-quality Pareto frontier, simultaneously delivering higher speedup and better fidelity than prior cache-based accelerators. 

Future Works. Several directions remain open. First, the closed-loop principle behind AlignCache is not specific to caching, the same counterfactual error measurement could calibrate other training-free accelerators such as token pruning or sparse attention. Second, current calibration treats each prompt independently; learning a prompt-conditioned prior over ¯rp,t could further reduce warm-up overhead and unlock higher speedups on short trajectories. Third, combining AlignCache with orthogonal 9acceleration techniques such as quantization, distillation, or compiler-level kernel fusion could yield compounded speedups while the closed-loop controller absorbs the additional approximation error introduced by these layers and preserves stable quality across heterogeneous deployment hardware. Finally, extending AlignCache to long-form video generation and multi-modal diffusion pipelines, where redundancy patterns differ substantially from text-to-video, is a natural next step toward a unified, self-correcting acceleration framework for generative models at scale.

A.8 Broader Impacts 

AlignCache reduces the inference cost of video diffusion models, lowering the energy footprint of large-scale deployment and improving accessibility for researchers with limited GPU resources. As a training-free acceleration layer, it inherits the dual-use risks of the underlying generators, faster inference could lower the barrier for misuse such as deepfakes or disinformation, and any biases of the base model are preserved. We therefore recommend deploying AlignCache alongside existing upstream safeguards, including content moderation, provenance watermarking, and the release policies of the original video generators. 15 Figure 6: Qualitative comparison on “A bird building a nest from twigs and leaves.” generated by Open-Sora-1.2.
